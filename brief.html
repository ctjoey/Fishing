Prompt 3The single biggest pain gap

1 · Phantom stock

The product page says In Stock, the card is charged at checkout, and the customer learns days or weeks later, usually by noticing nothing has shipped, that the item was backordered, drop-shipped from a vendor who doesn't have it, or never really there.

2 · Evidence

  • Present on all ten sites plus Omnia. Every other theme is on fewer, and most of them are downstream of this one.
  • Retailers have institutionalized it. Bass Pro, Cabela's and Sportsman's publish help articles titled "Why was my backorder / order canceled?". TackleDirect's FAQ says backorders unfilled in 30 days are cancelled. Omnia's terms reserve the right to cancel when "availability information on the website is incorrect." Scheels' public reply says it "cannot guarantee items will be on the sales floor."
  • The harm is time-bound and unrefundable.
"Nowhere on the website does it mention that any fishing poles won't ship for weeks, absolute waste of time and ruined a fishing trip."Karl's Bait & Tackle, Trustpilot
"Once you pay and wait 24 to 48 hours for the tracking, the product mysteriously becomes sold out, even though the product is still available for sale online."Scheels, Trustpilot
"We still have your scale set aside, we're just not prioritizing back orders and we are getting new orders out."Tackle Warehouse staff, quoted on BBC Boards
"They should have the product before taking payment."TackleDirect, Trustpilot, after 16 business days
  • Money is held meanwhile: 7 weeks (Cabela's), 4+ months (Tackle Warehouse), 7 months (TackleDirect pre-order), 2 months (TackleDirect, Karl's).

3 · Root cause

The "In Stock" badge is not a promise from a warehouse. It is the union of four inventory sources with different reliability, all rendered as the same green text:

  • Store-floor fulfillment (Scheels, Sportsman's, Bass Pro/Cabela's): a walk-in customer can buy the unit between checkout and pick.
  • Drop-ship SKUs (Discount Tackle, Omnia, TackleDirect, Academy marketplace): the retailer shows a vendor feed that is stale or optimistic.
  • Backorder-as-purchase (Tackle Warehouse, TackleDirect, Cabela's): checkout accepts and charges for units that exist only in an inbound purchase order.
  • Charge on order, not on ship: with the card already captured, nothing forces the retailer to say anything until its own cancellation window expires.

4 · Why it's missed

  • Every party sees an edge case. Each retailer's data says most orders ship fine, so the fix stays a help-center article. Review sites bucket it under "shipping" or "customer service," hiding that it is one specific cross-retailer failure.
  • Existing tools solve the opposite problem. Back-in-stock alerters (Visualping, NowInStock, Distill, retailers' own "notify me") tell you when a page says an item is available. Nothing tells you whether that claim has been true for other buyers. Price trackers compare price, never fulfillment.
  • Reputation scores are too coarse. A 1.8-star rating mixes gun-counter rudeness, warranty disputes and shipping. An angler choosing between two shops for a reel needed by Friday cannot extract "will this ship this week" from either.
  • Nobody is incentivized to publish it. Retailers won't advertise cancel rates; marketplaces publish ship-rates only for their own sellers; carriers only see a parcel once it exists.

5 · Opportunity

An independent, per-retailer ships-as-promised rate, built from buyers' own order outcomes, answering one question before checkout: when this retailer says In Stock, how often does it ship with tracking within three business days?

  • Smallest useful unit is the retailer-level rate plus a documented-incident count. SKU and category rates are the obvious next step, not a prerequisite.
  • Wedge: tournament and trip-driven anglers, who feel the cost most and already trade this information informally on forums.
  • Moat: the outcome log compounds, retailers can't publish it without disclosing it, and general review sites have no field for "days to tracking."
  • Deliberately out of scope for the first product: a browser extension on the product page, retailer-side "charge on ship" badges, automatic capture from order-confirmation emails.
